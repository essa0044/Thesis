# Notes, 12 Oct 2026

## Who does the low-level control?

- **PX4 on the flight controller** (the Pixhawk 6C in the X500 kit). It reads the IMU, runs the attitude and rate control loops, and drives the four motors through the ESCs.
- Our Jetson only sends **velocity setpoints** over MAVLink in OFFBOARD mode. The safety pilot can switch OFFBOARD off on the RC at any time.
- **Same split as the TU Delft paper.** Their spiking network turned events into optical flow. A small evolved controller turned that into thrust and attitude commands, and **PX4 on a Pixhawk 4 Mini** turned those into motor speeds, at about 200 Hz on Intel Loihi. We command one level higher (velocity) because our SNN is a reflex, not a full controller.
- Wording for the thesis: SNN = **spiking** neural network.

## How the simulation handles weight, cameras, the SNN and the VLM

1. **Weight and inertia.** PX4's simulated X500 (Gazebo) is a model file with a 2.0 kg body plus 4 rotors of 16 g. We set it to our estimated all-up weight (1.68 kg, so 1.617 kg body) and scale the inertia. The numbers are on the "Weight & flight time" sheet. PX4 then flies a drone as heavy as ours, with higher hover thrust and slower reactions.
2. **Flight time and payload** come from that sheet, not from the simulation. PX4's simulated battery only counts down on a timer. Estimates: about 11 minutes of hover, thrust-to-weight about 2.7, and 0.42 kg of payload out of the kit's ~1 kg limit.
3. **Cameras.** Gazebo renders colour and depth images, and PX4 even ships an X500 with an OAK-D Lite depth camera. For the event camera, the rendered video is converted to events with v2e, or with neurodrone's own synthetic event generator. TU Delft trained their controller in simulation without any events at all.
4. **SNN and VLM.** They run as real software in the loop. Their speed and power on the Jetson are measured on the bench (experiments 5.1 and 5.4) and fed back into the simulation as latency. A later step is hardware-in-the-loop, where the Jetson runs the real stack against the simulated drone.
