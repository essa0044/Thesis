# Abstract {.unnumbered}

*Draft — to be rewritten when the hardware results are in.*

Small drones that can be told in plain language what to look for ("fly to the red box") need two things that pull in opposite directions: an open-vocabulary perception model, which today means a vision-language model (VLM) with billions of parameters, and fast, reliable reactions to obstacles, which a VLM answering every few seconds cannot provide. This thesis studies how such a system can run on board a standard PX4 quadrotor with an 8 GB NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano and an Intel RealSense D435i depth camera. The VLM only selects the goal: it returns a 2-D bounding box, the depth image turns the box into a 3-D goal, and a goal is accepted only when two of three answers agree. A classical depth-camera guard and a reactive go-around keep the drone away from obstacles independently of the VLM.

Small open VLMs were compared for grounding on photographs (RefCOCO, gRefCOCO) and on drone images (RefDrone). Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct at 4-bit quantisation (3.3 GB) found 69 % of the targets on drone images, the same as models eight times its size, at about 0.6 s per image on a workstation GPU; quantisation to 4 bit cost the 4B and 8B models nothing measurable. In 60 closed-loop runs in PX4 software-in-the-loop with Gazebo, the complete stack reached the named target in 57 runs without a collision; an active-sensing search that chooses the next view from the VLM's answers needed fewer VLM answers than a fixed sweep when the target started at the side the sweep looks at second, and the same number otherwise. The remaining failures came from two limits of the design: the depth inside a box can belong to an object in front of the target, and the reactive go-around has no memory of obstacles that have left the camera's view. The deployment on the real quadrotor follows a staged plan from a VLM bench test on the Jetson to flights in the lab; its results will complete this thesis.
